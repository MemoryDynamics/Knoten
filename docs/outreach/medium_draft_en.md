As a small child, I dreamed that I was flying freely. Then I would turn a
little and get caught in my own flight path. I became so entangled in it
that I was trapped in complex knots. That moment felt almost like a
nightmare.

I often found a way out. Once I was finally free, flying freely would
eventually become boring again. I would turn a little, and the whole thing
would start over.

This memory is deeply personal. It explains much of my motivation for the
project: my movement left something behind that I then became caught in.
My own past affected what happened next. That image was the starting point
for the idea of a trajectory equation with memory, and later for the
question of interactions.

Whether this can become a sound scientific idea depends on precise
definitions, calculations, counterexamples, and reproducible tests. The
memory itself is not a finding about physics.

The larger question behind my project is this: could persistent structures
arise from a simple dynamics with memory? And could such an approach
eventually contribute to our understanding of physical structures? A
“theory of everything” names a far-reaching hope. What exists today is a
limited mathematical research model.

## From the Dream to Dynamics with Memory

The path to the current formulation developed gradually through
philosophical questions and the search for precise concepts. What is a
state? What role does the past play? What would a description need to
contain to determine the next step, or to specify its probability?

This also led us to the Markov property. In the model, the visible position
alone generally does not determine the distribution of the next step: the
stored past contributes as well. Including the full memory state provides
the appropriate Markov description. This makes the question mathematically
more precise; it does not, by itself, establish a law of nature.

The project is called **Emergenz Knoten**. In simple terms, a point moves,
leaves a fading trace, and responds to its stored past. The fading of the
trace and its feedback on the motion are described by specified rules. The
computer retains a finite number of past positions.

I am interested in whether this feedback can support persistent forms, and
under what conditions they disappear. This requires checking whether a
striking structure comes from the proposed mechanism or is already
explained by a simpler reference model.

The project name also needs a qualification: “knots” is initially a working
term. A circular orbit is not yet a topological knot, much less a
demonstrated elementary particle.

## What We Can Say So Far

In the stochastic branch of the model, there are controlled findings about
a co-moving memory cloud. A linear relaxation description explains key
properties. This limits the stronger interpretation that a distinct
nonlinear particle mechanism has already been found.

A separate deterministic branch examines prepared circular motion. For six
specified parameter cells, local existence and uniqueness certificates are
available under the documented interval-arithmetic assumptions. In a later
test with 2,400 stored positions, spectral calculations and three nonlinear
perturbation trajectories provide consistent numerical evidence of local
stability.

The word *local* matters here. The test does not establish global stability
or provide a complete spectral proof. A separately certified local root of
the infinite-memory equation and the finite-memory stability finding also
do not yet establish a continuous, verified connection to infinite memory.

## Where the Larger Story Remains Unsupported

These model findings do not yet establish physical mass, charge, spin,
quantum mechanics, or real spacetime. Whether the model can support a sound
connection to physics at all remains open. Such a connection would require,
among other things, independently testable predictions and a relationship
to measurable quantities.

Technical failures also need to be described accurately. Three attempts at
a planned interaction analysis failed in the results pipeline. They provide
neither a positive nor a negative scientific interaction finding. A run
that leaves no usable results file does not settle the research question.

Making these limits visible is part of working on the idea. A simpler
countermodel or a reproducible error can be more valuable than another
visually appealing simulation.

## Another Question: Memory, Neural Patterns, and AI

Perhaps the dream has more to do with neural processes than with the
structure of the physical world. The idea that I might, in some sense,
have experienced my own neurons forming connections is a personal
interpretation. That process cannot be identified or demonstrated from
the dream recollection.

For me, this opens a second research question: could an artificial dynamics
in which its own past influences subsequent motion enable interesting
memory or learning mechanisms? That would be a separate question for AI
development, one that could be investigated without a physical theory of
everything.

At present, this establishes neither an explanation of the brain nor
learning ability or usefulness for AI. Such a claim would need its own
defined tasks, metrics, and comparisons with simpler models. A memory term
alone does not demonstrate learning.

## Why I Want to Open the Discussion

The project has grown too large for me. At the same time, I find it
increasingly difficult to find people who have time for a review.

I would like to make it easier to get involved. Nobody should have to
evaluate an entire world model first. A particular derivation, a
reproduction step, or a clearly stated objection is enough to contribute.
Anyone motivated by a persistent fundamental question is welcome. Shared
enthusiasm can start a collaboration; the results must also withstand
skeptical scrutiny.

A Medium article can make this invitation visible. GitHub keeps the
equations, code, findings, and changes together in a traceable record.
Specialist journals remain a possible publication route for clearly
bounded results. The open discussion can begin earlier.

My request is this: help establish which parts hold up, which admit simpler
explanations, and which should be discarded.

The
[invitation to contribute a review](https://github.com/MemoryDynamics/Knoten/blob/main/docs/outreach/contribute_review_en.md)
offers three concrete starting points. Feedback can be recorded as a
[GitHub issue](https://github.com/MemoryDynamics/Knoten/issues/new?template=focused_review_en.md)
with a specific claim and an explanation that others can follow. A
well-supported “that does not follow” is helpful too.

The dream explains why I ask the question. The answer remains open.
